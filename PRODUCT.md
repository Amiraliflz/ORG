# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary:** General Iranian travelers — families, commuters, students — booking intercity taxi rides and airport transfers. They use the site when planning a specific journey: origin known, destination known, date in mind. They arrive with moderate digital literacy, often on mobile, sometimes under time pressure. Trust is their primary filter; they want reassurance before they hand over payment.

**Secondary:** Travel agencies and organizational buyers (AgencyArea) who reserve seats on behalf of customers. They work faster, expect power-user density, and care about throughput over discovery.

## Product Purpose

MrShoofer connects Iranian travelers with vetted intercity taxis and airport transfers. The product exists to replace the anxiety and friction of arranging a long-distance ride — unknown drivers, unclear pricing, no confirmation — with a single structured flow that ends in a ticket the passenger can rely on.

Success looks like: a traveler books a confirmed seat in under two minutes, feels no doubt about what they purchased, and boards the vehicle without surprises.

## Positioning

The safest intercity ride in Iran — not the cheapest, not the fastest to book, but the one where nothing goes wrong.

## Brand Personality

Calm authority. The brand doesn't shout or rush. It states, confirms, and delivers. Personality in three words: **reliable, clear, present**. Emotionally, the interface should feel the way a good road feels — smooth, predictable, without drama.

## Anti-references

**Loud promo sites** — anything with stacked discount badges, animated banners, countdown timers, or urgency copy ("Only 2 seats left!"). That register signals desperation and erodes the trust the product depends on. If an element could appear on a deals aggregator, it doesn't belong here.

## Design Principles

1. **Safety signals over sales signals.** Every screen earns trust before asking for action. Confirmation and clarity come first; conversion is the outcome, not the cue.
2. **Restraint as authority.** The absence of noise is itself a message: this brand is confident enough not to shout. Use space deliberately; decoration is earned.
3. **Persian-first, not adapted.** RTL is not a flip of an LTR layout. Typography, rhythm, icon direction, and reading flow are designed from the right, for a Persian reader, from the start.
4. **The path is the product.** Booking intercity travel is the core job. The search → select → confirm → pay flow is the interface; everything else is support.
5. **Consistency breeds trust.** Repeated patterns (same card style, same button behavior, same confirmation shape) reduce cognitive load and signal a well-run operation. Inconsistency feels like a company that doesn't have its act together.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Persian text at body sizes must hit ≥4.5:1 against background. All interactive elements keyboard-navigable. Reduced-motion alternative for any animation. Persian digit normalization handled server-side (already implemented).
